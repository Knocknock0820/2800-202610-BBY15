# Snake Plant Care Guide

## Overview

**Snake Plant** (*Sansevieria* or *Dracaena trifasciata*) is a hardy indoor plant known for its upright sword-shaped leaves and low-maintenance care requirements.

It is one of the most beginner-friendly houseplants because it:

- Tolerates low light
- Requires little watering
- Survives dry indoor environments
- Grows slowly and steadily
- Is difficult to kill compared to many other houseplants

Snake plants are native to tropical regions of West Africa and are popular in homes and offices because of their clean and modern appearance.

---

# Basic Care Guide

## Light

Snake plants can survive in many lighting conditions.

### Best Conditions:
- Bright indirect light
- Medium indoor light

### Can Tolerate:
- Low light areas
- Artificial office lighting

### Avoid:
- Long periods of harsh direct sunlight

More light usually helps the plant grow faster.

---

## Water

Snake plants prefer dry soil and are very sensitive to overwatering.

### Watering Tips:
- Let the soil dry completely before watering again
- Water less during winter
- Always use pots with drainage holes

### General Rule:
It is safer to underwater than overwater a snake plant.

### Common Signs:
| Symptom | Possible Cause |
|---|---|
| Yellow soft leaves | Overwatering |
| Wrinkled leaves | Underwatering |
| Mushy roots | Root rot |

---

## Humidity & Temperature

Snake plants adapt well to normal indoor conditions.

### Preferred Temperature:
- 18–29°C (65–85°F)

### Humidity:
- Normal household humidity is fine
- Does not require misting

Avoid exposing the plant to freezing temperatures.

---

## Soil

Snake plants need well-draining soil.

### Recommended Soil:
- Cactus or succulent mix
- Potting soil mixed with perlite or sand

Poor drainage may lead to root rot.

---

## Growth & Maintenance

Snake plants grow upright and spread slowly by underground rhizomes.

### Maintenance Tips:
- Remove damaged leaves near the base
- Rotate the pot occasionally for even growth
- Repot only when the plant becomes root-bound

Snake plants usually grow slowly indoors.

---

# Common Problems

| Problem | Possible Cause |
|---|---|
| Yellow soft leaves | Overwatering |
| Brown leaf tips | Dry air or inconsistent watering |
| Mushy base | Root rot |
| Leaning leaves | Insufficient light or overcrowded roots |

---

# Pet Safety

⚠️ Snake plants are mildly toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Keep the plant away from pets that chew leaves.

---

# Beginner Tips

If you are new to houseplants:

- Place the snake plant near a window with indirect light
- Water only when the soil is completely dry
- Avoid watering too frequently

Snake plants are extremely forgiving and are excellent for beginners or busy people.
