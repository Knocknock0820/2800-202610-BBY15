# Monstera Care Guide

## Overview

**Monstera deliciosa**, commonly called the _Swiss cheese plant_, is a tropical houseplant known for its large green leaves with natural holes and splits called **fenestrations**.

Monsteras are popular indoor plants because they:

- Grow relatively fast
- Look decorative and dramatic
- Adapt well to indoor environments
- Can become very large with proper care

In nature, Monsteras grow in the tropical rainforests of Mexico and Central America. They are climbing plants that naturally attach themselves to trees.

---

# Basic Care Guide

## Light

Monsteras grow best in **bright indirect light**.

### Recommended:

- Near an east-facing window
- A few feet away from a bright south-facing window

### Avoid:

- Strong direct afternoon sunlight
- Very dark corners

Too much direct sunlight may burn the leaves.

---

## Water

Water the plant when the top 2–5 cm (1–2 inches) of soil feels dry.

### Tips:

- Use pots with drainage holes
- Do not leave the pot sitting in water
- Reduce watering during winter

### Common Signs:

| Symptom         | Possible Cause                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Yellow leaves   | Overwatering                   |
| Drooping leaves | Underwatering or root problems |
| Curling leaves  | Dry soil or dry air            |

---

## Humidity & Temperature

Monsteras prefer:

- Temperature: **18–29°C (65–85°F)**
- Moderate to high humidity

Dry winter air may cause brown edges on leaves. A humidifier or light misting can help.

---

## Soil

Use loose, well-draining soil.

### A good mix can include:

- Potting soil
- Perlite
- Orchid bark or coco chips

Good airflow around the roots helps prevent root rot.

---

## Support & Growth

Monsteras are climbing plants and grow better with support.

### Recommended supports:

- Moss pole
- Wooden stake
- Trellis

Providing support often helps the plant grow larger leaves with more splits.

### Aerial Roots

The brown aerial roots are normal.

You can:

- Leave them alone
- Guide them into the soil
- Attach them to a moss pole

---

# Common Problems

| Problem            | Possible Cause                         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Yellow leaves      | Overwatering                           |
| Brown crispy edges | Low humidity or underwatering          |
| No leaf splits     | Not enough light or plant is too young |
| Drooping leaves    | Water stress or root issues            |
| Black mushy stems  | Root rot                               |

---

# Pet Safety

Monsteras are mildly toxic to cats and dogs if chewed.

Keep the plant away from pets that like biting plants.

---

# Beginner Tips

If you are new to indoor plants:

- Start with a medium-sized Monstera
- Use a pot with drainage holes
- Check the soil before watering instead of following a strict schedule

Monsteras are generally forgiving plants and show visible signs when something is wrong.
