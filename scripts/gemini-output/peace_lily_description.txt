# Botanical Care Guide: *Spathiphyllum* (Peace Lily)

The **Peace Lily** (*Spathiphyllum*) is a popular indoor houseplant known for its elegant white spathes and lush, dark green foliage. Native to the tropical rainforests of the Americas, this plant is favored for its air-purifying qualities and its ability to thrive in lower-light environments.

---

## Overview
*   **Botanical Name:** *Spathiphyllum spp.*
*   **Family:** Araceae
*   **Plant Type:** Tropical perennial
*   **Mature Size:** 1–4 feet tall and wide, depending on the cultivar.
*   **Growth Habit:** Clumping, upright foliage with seasonal blooming.

---

## Light
Peace Lilies are remarkably adaptable, but they perform best in **bright, indirect light**.
*   **Optimal:** A spot near a north- or east-facing window.
*   **Low Light:** They can tolerate lower light levels, though this may result in fewer blooms and a slower growth rate.
*   **Avoid:** Direct, harsh sunlight. Exposure to direct sun will scorch the leaves, causing yellow or brown patches.

---

## Water
The Peace Lily is famous for its dramatic "wilting" when thirsty, which serves as a helpful reminder for the owner.
*   **Schedule:** Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil feel dry to the touch. 
*   **Technique:** Keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged. Ensure the pot has adequate drainage holes to prevent root rot.
*   **Water Quality:** Peace Lilies are sensitive to chlorine and fluoride found in tap water. If your plant develops brown tips, try using filtered, distilled, or rainwater.

---

## Soil
To thrive, *Spathiphyllum* requires a potting medium that balances moisture retention with excellent aeration.
*   **Mix:** A high-quality, peat-based potting mix is ideal. 
*   **Amendments:** Incorporate perlite or coarse sand to improve drainage and prevent soil compaction around the root system. 
*   **pH Level:** Prefers slightly acidic soil (pH 5.8–6.5).

---

## Common Problems

| Problem | Potential Cause | Recommended Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yellowing Leaves** | Overwatering or poor drainage | Allow soil to dry out; check for root rot. |
| **Brown Leaf Tips** | Low humidity or chemical sensitivity | Increase humidity; use distilled water. |
| **Lack of Blooms** | Insufficient light | Move to a brighter location with indirect light. |
| **Drooping Leaves** | Dehydration | Water thoroughly; plant will recover in hours. |
| **Pests (Scale/Aphids)** | Environmental stress | Wipe leaves with neem oil or insecticidal soap. |

---

## Pet Safety
**Warning:** The Peace Lily is **toxic to both cats and dogs.**

*   **Toxin:** The plant contains **insoluble calcium oxalate crystals**.
*   **Symptoms:** If ingested, these crystals cause immediate irritation of the mouth, tongue, and throat, leading to excessive drooling, difficulty swallowing, vomiting, and pawing at the mouth.
*   **Recommendation:** Keep this plant out of reach of pets. If ingestion is suspected, contact your veterinarian immediately.
