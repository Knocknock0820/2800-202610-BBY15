# Plant Care Guide: Peace Lily (*Spathiphyllum*)

The **Peace Lily** is a popular, elegant indoor plant known for its lush, dark green foliage and signature white, hood-like spathes. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, it is highly valued for its ability to thrive in low-light conditions and its reputation as an effective air-purifying houseplant.

---

## Overview
*   **Scientific Name:** *Spathiphyllum*
*   **Plant Type:** Tropical evergreen perennial
*   **Native Region:** Tropical regions of the Americas and Southeast Asia
*   **Mature Size:** 1–4 feet tall (depending on the cultivar)
*   **Difficulty:** Easy/Beginner-friendly

---

## Light
Peace Lilies are prized for their ability to tolerate **low-light conditions**, making them ideal for offices or rooms with limited natural light. 
*   **Optimal Light:** Bright, **indirect sunlight**. 
*   **Avoid:** Direct, harsh sun, which can scorch the leaves and cause brown, crispy patches. 
*   **Indicator:** If your plant is not flowering, it is likely not receiving enough light. Move it to a slightly brighter location to encourage blooms.

---

## Water
Peace Lilies are "drama queens"—they will visibly wilt when they are thirsty, but they typically recover quickly once watered.
*   **Frequency:** Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil feel dry to the touch. 
*   **Technique:** Use filtered or distilled water if possible, as Peace Lilies are sensitive to chemicals like **fluoride and chlorine** found in tap water, which can cause brown leaf tips.
*   **Humidity:** As tropical plants, they appreciate high humidity. Mist the leaves regularly or place the plant on a pebble tray filled with water to increase ambient moisture.

---

## Soil
A well-draining, moisture-retentive potting mix is essential to prevent root rot while keeping the plant hydrated.
*   **Preferred Medium:** A high-quality peat-based potting mix amended with perlite or orchid bark to improve aeration.
*   **pH Level:** Slightly acidic to neutral (5.8–6.5).
*   **Drainage:** Always ensure your pot has drainage holes to prevent the roots from sitting in standing water.

---

## Common Problems

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Brown Leaf Tips** | Fluoride in water or low humidity | Switch to distilled water; mist leaves regularly. |
| **Yellow Leaves** | Overwatering or poor drainage | Check soil moisture; ensure pot has drainage holes. |
| **Drooping Leaves** | Dehydration | Water thoroughly; the plant should perk up within hours. |
| **Pests (Scale/Aphids)** | Dry air or poor airflow | Wipe leaves with neem oil or insecticidal soap. |
| **No Flowers** | Insufficient light | Move to a brighter location with indirect light. |

---

## Pet Safety
**Warning:** The Peace Lily is **toxic to both cats and dogs.** 

The plant contains **insoluble calcium oxalate crystals**. If ingested, these crystals can cause:
*   Intense oral irritation and burning of the mouth, tongue, and lips.
*   Excessive drooling and difficulty swallowing.
*   Vomiting.

**Recommendation:** Keep this plant out of reach of curious pets. If you suspect your pet has ingested any part of a Peace Lily, contact your veterinarian immediately.
